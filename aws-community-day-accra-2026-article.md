# Building the Backbone: My AWS Community Day Accra Story

I applied to speak at AWS Community Day, Accra, and when the acceptance email came in, I was thrilled. This was just a week after I had attended the AWS Summit in Johannesburg, so I went straight from one AWS event into preparing for another. It kept me busy, but I wasn't about to turn down the chance to speak in Accra.

Accra was a double first for me: my first time in Ghana, and my first visit to any country in West Africa. I had been to other parts of the continent before, but West Africa was new ground, and I was curious to see how the tech community there compared to what I know from East Africa.

## A Ghana Connection That Started in 2021

Ghana already meant something to me before I booked my ticket. Back in May 2021, I was part of the first cohort of the AWS re/Start program with Azubi Africa. Those classes ran online, with learners joining in from different countries, and Ghana had one of the biggest groups in our cohort. We spent months together in that program, working through labs, group assignments, and late study sessions, all through a screen.

I never got to meet most of those Ghanaian classmates face to face. We built friendships and exchanged encouragement in a WhatsApp group, but a laptop screen only takes a connection so far. I always hoped I would meet some of them in person one day, and this trip finally gave me that chance. Knowing that reunion was waiting for me in Accra made the whole trip feel personal from the start, not just another speaking engagement.

## Getting There

With my talk confirmed, I booked my flight and accommodation. The event was set for a Saturday at the University of Ghana, so I planned to arrive a few days early to settle in, get over the travel, and prepare properly instead of rushing in the night before.

I left home on Thursday. The flight took about seven hours with a connection through Lomé, Togo. Sitting in the airport during that layover, I remember thinking about how much ground I was covering, from East Africa all the way to the western edge of the continent, and how AWS events keep giving me reasons to see more of Africa than I would on my own. I landed in Accra around 3 p.m. West Africa time, three hours behind East Africa, so my body clock needed a moment to catch up. From the airport, I went straight to my hotel to rest and get ready for the days ahead.

The next day, I worked on my presentation and demo, going through my slides one more time and testing my setup so nothing would go wrong on stage. Once I was satisfied with the prep, I took a walk around the area near my hotel just to get a feel for the city. Accra had its own pace and its own noise, different from what I'm used to, and I liked that I had a full day to just take it in without being rushed.

I also tried Ghanaian food for the first time, jollof rice, banku, and a few other dishes. Every West African country seems to have its own version of jollof, and people can get quite passionate defending theirs, so I made sure to pay attention while I ate. It was a good way to settle into the city before the big day, and honestly, a nice break from thinking about slides and demos for a few hours.

## The Day Arrives

Saturday came, and I woke up early, ready to be part of it. The theme for this AWS Community Day was "Building the Backbone: Powering Ghana's Digital Ambitions Through Cloud Technology." Looking back, that theme captured exactly what the day turned out to be. It wasn't only about the technology on display; it was about the community that keeps showing up to build it together.

Walking onto the University of Ghana campus that morning, I could already feel the energy. Volunteers were directing people to registration, early arrivals were finding their seats, and there was that particular buzz you only get on the morning of an event that people have been looking forward to for weeks.

The event had strong backing from partners who invest in local tech talent: [JetBrains](https://www.linkedin.com/company/jetbrains/), [AmaliTech](https://www.linkedin.com/company/amalitech/), [Seevcash](https://www.linkedin.com/company/seevcash/), and [Paystack](https://www.linkedin.com/company/paystack/). Events like this don't run on goodwill alone. Someone has to cover the venue, the food, the branding, the small logistics that attendees never think about but would notice immediately if they were missing. Talking to the partner representatives, I got the sense that they weren't just writing checks and walking away. They were genuinely interested in the people in the room and what the community would build next.

## My Talk: Agent Evaluation and Decision Engines

My session covered agent evaluation and decision engines, a topic I've been spending a lot of time on lately as more teams move from experimenting with AI agents to actually trusting them with real decisions. I walked the audience through four evaluation dimensions that I believe teams should be checking before they let an agent run on its own, then took a closer look at routing agents and decision patterns, since that's usually where things get complicated once you move past a single simple agent.

For the demo, I invoked Strands Agents on Runtime and compared that with a LangGraph agent running on AgentCore Runtime. I wanted the audience to see the two approaches side by side rather than just hear me describe them, so they could judge for themselves where each one made sense. It was a technical session, but the questions afterward told me people were following closely and already thinking about how to use this in their own projects. A few people stayed back after the talk to ask more, which is always a good sign that a session landed well.

## Talking With the Community

Chatting with community members during the day, I noticed many of them are still early in their AWS journey. A lot of them are curious and want to go deeper into cloud but haven't had many chances to do so yet. Some asked me how I got started, others wanted advice on certifications, and a few just wanted to know how someone builds a career around community work like this. It's a good reminder of why community days matter: they give people who are ready to learn an actual place to start, and someone to ask the questions they can't easily find answers to online.

One conversation that stood out was with the Seevcash team. They work on cross-border payments and offer digital wallets and money management tools, having started in 2022. What stood out to me was how they connect regular currency with Web3 assets, aiming to cut down both the cost and the time it usually takes to move money across borders compared to older remittance options. Cross-border payments are a real pain point across Africa, so it was good to see a local company tackling it head-on instead of waiting for someone else to solve it.

## Heading Home

By the end of the day, I had gotten far more out of it than I expected: new connections, old friends finally met in person, and a clearer sense of where the Ghanaian AWS community is headed. I flew back home the next day, tired but glad I made the trip.

Trips like this remind me why I keep saying yes to speaking opportunities, even when the travel is long and the schedule is tight. It's never just about standing on a stage. It's about the people you meet before and after, the conversations that happen in hallways and over food, and the small moments that end up mattering more than the talk itself.

A big thank you to the AWS Accra team, Nathaniel and Thelma, for pulling off such a strong first Community Day. Running a first-time event is never easy, and it showed in how well everything came together. Here's to more community days ahead, and more chances to learn, connect, and build together across Africa.
